\section{Question and controlled measurements}
\label{sec:controls}
An image group can be easier because it contains more open water or less vegetation, even when the groups are called clear and cloudy.
Our question is therefore what a clear--cloudy gap measures, rather than whether a new segmenter wins a benchmark.
The study uses released WorldFloods data and checkpoints~\cite{worldfloods,prithvi}, and stored scene-level confusions with locked analysis rules.
We report a composition control, a same-tile control and the aggregator together; none can substitute for the other two.

For predictions $P$, binary water labels $W$ and an explicitly specified valid scoring surface $V$, water IoU is $TP/(TP+FP+FN)$ on $V$.
Undefined denominators and insufficient labelled water are recorded as exclusions, rather than assigned perfect or zero scores.
The natural contrast for scoring unit $s$ is
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_s=100\,[\mathrm{IoU}_{s,\mathrm{clear}}-\mathrm{IoU}_{s,\mathrm{cloudy}}].
\end{equation}
The \textbf{composition control} compares unmatched tiles, then greedy within-unit pairs with absolute water-fraction difference at most 0.05, then pairs also satisfying an absolute mean clear-pixel NDVI difference at most 0.05.
The clear and cloudy groups must each contain at least 200 labelled water pixels.
Missing NDVI, unmatched tiles and dropped units remain visible in the accounting.

The \textbf{same-tile control} holds the target tile, transplanted mask and unpainted scoring surface fixed while comparing original predictions with constant cloud and non-cloud fills.
The paired cloud-minus-placebo contrast subtracts the two IoU drops on the identical scoring surface.
Painting is an intervention on the model input, not a physical cloud model or an independent water label beneath clouds.
Both fills are constructed fills on clear tiles, scored on unpainted pixels; they are not natural cloud.

The \textbf{aggregator} is the unweighted mean of eligible unit-level values, after pooling confusion within each unit.
It is named separately from pixel-pooled confusion and from any event-weighted point estimate.
Scene-bootstrap intervals use 5,000 draws, with seed 0 for composition and 42 for paste.
Activation-clustered follow-ups resample complete activations, retaining all included scenes and the same stored scene-mean point.
Their intervals do not change the statistic into an event-weighted mean.
No new decision threshold is introduced for the follow-ups.
